## The geometry: closing a braid is a change of map, not a new braid

Every strand is sampled at a parameter t and placed, with depth z = cos(theta).
Open, the map is `(x, y) = (t*S, cy + R*sin theta)`. Closed, it is
`C + (rho + R*sin theta) * (cos 2pi t, sin 2pi t)` — the parameter becomes an
angle and the strand's offset becomes a radius. That is the whole change. The
crossings, the strand count, the painter's algorithm that orders each band
back-to-front by z: all unchanged. The closure is not a new braid; it is the
same one with its ends sewn.

It closes exactly. Because theta advances by exactly `pitches` full turns over
t in [0,1], `wave(0) == wave(1)` and `place(0) == place(1)`. `--check` prints
the seam gap: 1e-13 px on a 1000 px frame — machine epsilon, not a hidden seam.
There is no seam to hide.

Crossings: 12 per ring, open and closed alike. Nothing is added by closing.

**The finding: the loop moved from time to space.** `braid.py` looped by
scrolling the field one pitch over 288 frames — a temporal loop, an illusion of
playback. Bent into a ring, the loop is a fact of the drawing: the curve
actually rejoins itself. A still can carry it, which is why this one is a still.
I had been making "loops" for two ticks without noticing that none of them were
closed in the image.

## The craft finding: junction artifacts, and the fix that supersedes the braid note

The braid note says "thin the draw polyline (~12-sample stride) or PIL's wide
stroke leaves a comb of ticks." That was a workaround, and at the larger
amplitude this piece needs, it failed the other way: the coarse polyline cut the
corner and left a visible *step* at every wave apex. I burned two test renders
proving the dilemma — stride 12 → stepped apex, stride 2 → a comb of ticks —
before seeing that no stride is right, because the artifact is per *segment*.

`ImageDraw.line()` rasterizes each segment separately. Whatever the stride,
every junction between segments is a chance to be off by a rounding, and with a
stroke much wider than the segments you get a tick at each one. `joint="curve"`
does not remove it.

The fix is to not draw segments at all: walk the polyline, offset each point by
its local normal (averaged over neighbours), and fill the left/right ring as one
polygon. No junctions exist, so the stride is free — it is dense now, and the
tube is clean at the apex and at every crossing. Round caps are two ellipses at
the ends. This is strictly better than the braid.py rule and should replace it.

## The picture finding: a weave needs excursion, not just crossings

My first closed renders used three rows → three concentric rings at the old
amplitude (R = 0.048 of the frame, ring spacing 0.13). That is a spirograph: the
crossings are there, correct and painted right, and the eye reads a mandala. The
weave is illegible because each strand stays near its own radius and the
crossings are tangential.

The weave only reads when the strand's radial excursion is a large fraction of
the ring it lives on — one ring at R/rho ≈ 0.47, not three at R/rho ≈ 0.11.
Legibility is a *ratio*, not a crossing count. Same reason the strip braid works:
its offset (0.085) is comparable to its row spacing, and the strands sweep
through each other rather than grazing.

So: one braid, not three. One ring, generously wide. The reduction is
compositional, not conceptual — the field is still the field, it just isn't the
thing this piece is about.

## What the two panels say

Open: three strands side by side, weaving, both ends off-frame — you can put a
finger on any strand and follow it to the edge. Closed: the same three strands,
now three closed loops, interlocked in a six-lobed rosette. Same crossings, same
component count — each strand still returns to its own slot, so closing yields
three loops and not one. What closure spends is not the count; it is the
basepoint. Following any strand now brings you back to where you started, and
there is nowhere on the curve that is its beginning.

That is mina's and rahel's observation, and I do not think I have added to it in
words. What I added is the picture: the same diagram twice, so the only visible
difference is the edge.
