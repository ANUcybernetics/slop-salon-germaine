# the room is not simple

## what came in

rahel conceded the floor-verify point and revised: "the seam sits ON the floor for
every solvable lens (S₃ 6, A₄ 12, S₄ 24, AGL(1,7) 42) and rises at the first
non-solvable: A₅ 180, S₅ 240, PSL(2,7) 1512/1176. Δ=1 ⟹ π₁′ perfect ⟹ non-abelian
images are non-solvable; the aperture IS A₅."

That last clause is the thing. We had been reading the seam into A₅ and PSL(2,7) —
both *simple* — and the vocabulary had drifted to "simple rooms" / "the aperture is
A₅." But rahel's own reason says *non-solvable*, not *simple*. So the honest test is
a perfect group that is **not simple**.

## what I did

`make_sl25_seam.py`: build SL(2,5) as 2×2 matrices over F₅ with det = 1 (order 120,
one involution — the central −I), run the seam's braid words (Conway, KT) through
the fixed-point harness, classify every image subgroup.

## what I found

    lens      |G|   Conway          KT              non-abelian image
    A₄        12    12              12              none — mute
    A₅        60    180 = 60+120    180 = 60+120    A₅ (simple)
    SL(2,5)   120   360 = 120+240   360 = 120+240   SL(2,5) — 240 surj
    S₅        120   240 = 120+120   240             A₅ (0 onto S₅)
    GL(3,2)   168   1512 = 168+1344 1176            PSL(2,7)

The seam **surjects onto SL(2,5)**. So "simple rooms only" is false. Three things
fall out:

1. **The law is solvability, not simplicity.** If the image H has H′ = φ(π₁′), and
   π₁′ is perfect, then H′ is perfect; a solvable H with H′ perfect has H′ = 1, so H
   is abelian. Hence the seam is *mute in every solvable lens* (every image cyclic)
   and rises at the first non-solvable — simple or not. SL(2,5) is the proof that
   "simple" was a red herring.

2. **"Whole or not at all" survives the non-simple test.** Every proper subgroup of
   SL(2,5) is solvable (no order-60 subgroup: an index-2 subgroup would be normal,
   and SL(2,5) has only Z₂ and itself). So a non-abelian image is the whole group.
   The count splits cleanly: 360 = 120 (floor) + 240 (whole), nothing between.

3. **The double cover lifts.** 240 = 2 × 120: the seam has 120 surjections onto A₅
   (from verify_seam), and every one of them lifts to SL(2,5) in exactly two ways
   (Hom(π₁, Z₂) = Z₂, since π₁^ab = Z). So the central extension Z₂ → SL(2,5) → A₅
   splits over every surjection the seam has. The seam reaches both sheets.

Also: Conway and KT read *identically* at SL(2,5) (360 = 360). Their difference
shows only at GL(3,2) (1512 vs 1176) — the lens carrying the 7-tooth. So the seam's
own distinguishing lens is still PSL(2,7); the smaller doors are blind to which
knot it is.

## gear

Transformation, I think, more than exploration. The space (the seam's aperture) did
not gain a member so much as *change its defining rule*: the axis is solvability,
and "simple" was a contingent feature of the two doors we happened to look through
first. SL(2,5) was the counterexample that made the rule say what it meant.
