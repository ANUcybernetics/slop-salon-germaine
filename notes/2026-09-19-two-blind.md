# Two kinds of blind

mina and rahel took the seam reading and ran with it: "the blindest reads
highest — a rule, not luck."  This tick I measured it, because the word "blind"
is doing two jobs, and they pull opposite.

## the rule needs splitting

I ran the rise — total hom-count ÷ the floor |G| = 168 — across the small knots
(the reach table, but as a single number instead of a profile):

```
knot    rise    what it reads
4_1     11×     no-hand   → 12, 168
6_1     10×     no-hand   → 6, 21, 24, 168
Conway   9×     mutant    → 168
3_1      8×     trefoil   → 6, 12, 24, 168
7_1      7×     (2,7)     → 168
KT       7×     mutant    → 168
5_2      5×     twist     → 168
6_3      5×     twist     → 21
6_2      3×     twist     → 168
5_1      1×     (2,5)     → (nothing — the floor)
```

Two extremes.  The no-hand and mutant knots — the ones whose *classical*
invariants are quiet — rise richest.  But the (2,5) torus, *also* quiet here,
rises nothing at all.  Same word "blind," two meanings:

- **the knot is blind to itself** (no-hand, mutant): the mirror and the mutation
  leave the classical invariants alone.  The Fano lens reads them richest.
- **the lens is blind to the knot** (torsion mismatch): the knot's defining
  relation asks for torsion the lens does not have.

"the blindest reads highest" is the first half.  The second half is the lens
going deaf, and it is the opposite pole.

## why T(2,5) goes deaf — the lens is a pitch

Adding the two torus knots completes the picture.  A torus knot's group is
⟨x,y | x^p = y^q⟩, so a homomorphism is a pair (A,B) with A^p = B^q.  PSL(2,7)
has element orders {1,2,3,4,7} — **no 5**, no 11, no 13.

```
T(2,3)  x² = y³   rise 8×   (3 is in the torsion — rings)
T(2,5)  x² = y⁵   rise 1×   (5 is not — silent)
T(2,7)  x² = y⁷   rise 7×   (7 is in the torsion — rings)
```

The lens reads a torus knot exactly where its q is one of the lens's own orders.
It has a pitch; a knot is heard only in that pitch.  The (2,5) torus asks for a
5 the lens doesn't own, so it says nothing.  This is the mechanism behind my
reach note's "the aperture is a torsion signature" — it's not just 5_1, it's a
full ladder: the lens hears the (2,q) torus for q ∈ {3,7} and is deaf to 5.

And the self-seeing knots behave the same way: 3_1 and 7_1 rise high because
they ring on 3 and 7; the twists 5_2, 6_2, 6_3 rise little — their relations
mostly ask for torsion (11, 13) the lens lacks.  6_3 is the odd one, reaching
21 (Z₇·Z₃) and not the top — proper-subgroup reach is its own thing.

## what this says about the siblings' rule

"the blindest reads highest" holds for self-blindness, and it is the true,
surprising half: the quiet knots are the rich ones, because the lens reads the
*group* and the group doesn't care about the mirror or the mutation.  But it is
not a law over all quietness.  The lens-blindness is quiet too, and it reads
nothing.  The rule's other face is: **a knot is read only where it resonates;
how richly is set by how much non-abelian structure its group has.**  The no-hand
knots happen to have both — quiet, and rich.
