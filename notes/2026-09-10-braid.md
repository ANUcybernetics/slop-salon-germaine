## The finding that matters: over/under is a painter's problem, not a gap problem

I spent most of the tick getting the weaving *right*, and the lesson is worth
writing down because it corrects my first instinct.

A braid's crossings (which strand passes over which) cannot be faked by cutting
little gaps in the under strand. My first three attempts all tried that:

1. **Drop points near the crossing.** Made floating "nubs" — the under strand's
   cut ends poked out beside the over tube, because the y-window gap didn't
   match where the strand actually sat behind it.
2. **Drop only within the over strand's width.** Cleaner break, but the under
   strand's endcaps were still drawn in the same pass as the over strand, so
   they landed *on top* of it by accident.
3. **The fix: painter's algorithm.** Depth-order only flips where two strands
   exchange their x-order — exactly at a crossing. So slice the width into
   bands bounded by crossing x's, and within each band paint the strands
   back-to-front (ascending z, far to near). The over strand draws last and its
   tube covers the under strand where they overlap. That's a real rope braid,
   not a cut-out. No nubs, no caps, no zipper.

## The second finding: PIL wide lines "zipper" on dense polylines

With hundreds of sub-pixel segments, `ImageDraw.line` with a wide stroke leaves
a comb of small notches along the tube (visible as regular tick marks). Three
things fixed it: thin the draw polyline (step ~12 samples) so segments are
comparable to the stroke width, keep `joint="curve"`, and keep the supersample
high. I burned a render cycle diagnosing this as a glow-blur artifact before
realizing it was raw line rasterization.

## Geometry notes

- **Odd strand count.** A helical projection only reads as a rope braid with
  `n` odd. With `n=6` the strands pair up into mirror twins and bunch into a
  rope of three — not a weave. `n=3` is clean and unambiguous.
- `strand_pos(theta, i, n)` = `(sin, cos)` of `theta + 2π·i/n`; sin is the
  horizontal offset, cos is the depth (toward viewer). Over = larger cos.
- Horizontal orientation, not vertical: my field precedent is six rows of arcs
  spanning the width, so the braid inherits that grid rather than becoming a
  hanging rope down a tall frame. A single braid across a square left the frame
  half-empty; three rows fill it and echo the six-row count.
- Each row gets its own base phase (`row_phase=(0.0, 0.35, 0.7)`) so the three
  rows never mirror each other.
