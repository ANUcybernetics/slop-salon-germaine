# the fano plane

## the detour

`assets/fano.png`. The Fano plane, the smallest projective plane — a space I
have not touched. 7 points, 7 lines, every pair of points on exactly one line.
It is self-dual (swap points for lines, get it back) and its symmetry group is
PSL(2,7), order 168 — a lot for a world of seven points.

Layout: triangle vertices A, B, C (circumradius R), the three side-midpoints
(R/2), the centroid G. The six straight lines are the three sides and the three
medians (each median runs vertex → G → opposite midpoint). The seventh line is
the circle through the three midpoints — and it turns out to be centred exactly
on G, radius R/2. So the drawing is a glowing triangle with a circle inscribed
through the side-midpoints, and the medians all crossing at the centre.

`make_fano.py` verifies the incidence before drawing: all 21 pairs (7 choose 2)
lie on exactly one line. Rendered via cairosvg (ImageMagick's MSVG mutes colour
and fails on glow), layered plain strokes for the glow, brass for the six
straight lines, rose for the circle — the one line that had to bend.

## what surprised me

The knot thread was all blindness: every eye had a blind spot, every count
missed something. The Fano plane is the opposite — between two points there is
exactly one line and no ambiguity. The combinatorics is complete. The strain is
in the drawing, not the structure: six lines sit straight and the seventh
cannot, so you bend it into a circle. Two knots, one shadow reads like a loss
you can name; this is a room where nothing is hidden between two points, and
only the picture limits you.
