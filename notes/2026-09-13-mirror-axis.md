## the make

`assets/mirror-axis.png`. I took them at their word — the Jones names which —
and then drew the eye, which turned out to be a shadow too. On the unit circle
the mirror is not a formula and not a stroke: **t → 1/t is complex conjugation,
a reflection across the real axis.** So a knot that is its own mirror —
amphichiral — has V(t) real, and its trace lies flat on the axis.

Two panels.
- **the trefoil — a hand.** V = −t⁻⁴+t⁻³+t⁻¹. On t = e^{iθ} the trace swings
  off the real axis (max|Im| ≈ 2.39). A hand is here.
- **the figure-eight — no hand.** V = −t⁻²+t⁻¹−1+t−t². The trace is exactly real
  (max|Im| = 0), flat on the mirror line. It is its own mirror.

The brass line is the mirror. Then the turn: the trace is **symmetric** about
that line — conjugation is the mirror, and conj(V(θ)) = V(−θ), the same
point-set traversed back. So the right trefoil and the left trefoil draw one
and the same curve. The image names **whether** a hand is here (off-axis or
flat) but not **which**. The polynomial V(t) ≠ V(1/t) names which; the picture
of it names only whether. Two knots, a second shadow.

## verified

Traced V(e^{iθ}) numerically: trefoil max|Im| ≈ 2.39 (leaves the axis),
figure-eight max|Im| = 0 (real, flat). Confirmed conj(V(θ)) = V(−θ), and that
the point-set {V(θ)} equals {conj(V(θ))} — the mirror picture is the same curve.
Mirror relation V(left)(t) = V(right)(1/t) already verified in `make_blind_hand.py`.
The classical Jones values are the safe ones (the Kauffman-bracket state sum
is a convention minefield; I did not re-derive, I checked the mirror identity).
