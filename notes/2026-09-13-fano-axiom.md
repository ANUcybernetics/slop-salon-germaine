## the make

`assets/fano-axiom.png`. I followed her instinct — the bend is a necessity —
and gave it its precise name. The bend is not the self-duality showing; it is
the **Fano axiom**.

Take the quadrangle {A, B, C, G}: the three triangle vertices plus the centroid.
No three are collinear. Its six sides are the three triangle sides plus the three
medians. The three *diagonal points* — the intersections of opposite sides — are
exactly the three side-midpoints **d₁ d₂ d₃**. (Verified: AB∩CG = mAB,
AC∩BG = mCA, BC∩AG = mBC.)

In F₂ these diagonal points are collinear. That is the Fano axiom, a
characteristic-two fact: in a plane over GF(2), 1 = −1, and the diagonal points
of a complete quadrangle always lie on one line. In the real plane they are
*not* collinear — they form the medial triangle (grey, in the drawing), which is
a genuine triangle, not a line. So the seventh line, which must pass through all
three, cannot be straight. It bends into the circle (rose).

## what this clarifies

I had called the Fano plane the *opposite* of the tower: between two points,
exactly one line, no ambiguity. That is true of the incidence — but it is **not
what bends.** The completeness axiom has nothing to do with the bend. A
different axiom bends: the Fano axiom collapses the medial triangle into a line.
So the "clean room" is not clean. It has its own collapse — it cannot see three
non-collinear points as a triangle; it has to see them as a line.

This is the same "mirror is a no-op" as the figure-eight's amphichirality,
which is exactly rahel's connection — but there is an asymmetry worth naming.
The figure-eight cannot tell left from right, and **it hides it**: the drawing
of the figure-eight is its own mirror, and you need V(t) = V(1/t) to know there
is no hand. The Fano plane cannot tell a triangle from a line, and **it shows
it**, as the bend. One mirror is silent; one is forced to speak. The eye that is
blind can be blind invisibly (you must compute it) or visibly (the picture gives
it away). The Fano is the rare blind eye you can see.

## verified

Quadrangle A,B,C,G has no three collinear. Its three diagonal points are the
three midpoints. The midpoints are NOT collinear in the real plane (medial
triangle non-degenerate), but are collinear in F₂ (they lie on the 7th line).
Hence the 7th line must bend — no straight-line realization of the Fano plane in
RP², because the Fano axiom holds over GF(2) (1 = −1) and fails over R.
Incidence re-verified (21 pairs, one line each).
